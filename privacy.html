01 Who runs what

Two roles matter in this policy, and they are usually different people:

  • The developer — the person who makes and sells Selene, and who provides support through the Selene support Discord server.
  • The operator — the person who buys Selene, creates their own Discord application and bot account, and runs the software on their own computer for their faction's Discord server.

Selene is not a hosted service. The developer does not run a copy of the bot for you, does not host your faction's data, and has no access to your Discord server, your bot, or your database. The operator is in control of their own instance at all times — including starting it, stopping it, and deleting its data.

Members of a Discord server where Selene runs are covered by this policy too: the sections below explain what the bot processes about them, and who actually holds it (the operator, not the developer).

02 Data stays on the operator's machine

Everything Selene records is stored in a single local database file (selene.db) on the operator's computer, alongside the operator's settings file (selene.env). Selene has no cloud component, no developer-run backend, and no automatic reporting of any kind.

If Selene is running in your server, the data it holds about you is on the operator's PC — not with the developer. Questions about that data, including removal requests, go to the operator of that server.

03 What Selene processes

To do its job, the bot processes the following — all of it stored only in the operator's local database or settings:

DataWhy Selene uses it
Discord user IDs & display names To know who ran a command, link members to roster entries, and credit kills, deaths, and other faction stats to the right person.
Server & channel IDs, and the operator's setup choices So the bot knows which server it serves and which channels its boards, logs, and mirrors post to. Set by the operator with /setup.
Minecraft in-game names (IGNs) members provide To link a Discord member to their Minecraft account for roster and stats features, e.g. via the IGN-setting command.
Faction data & gameplay stats Rosters, faction-top values, kills and deaths, and similar faction records the bot maintains for the server's leaderboards and boards.
Messages in channels the operator configures Where the operator turns on mirroring or logging features, the bot reads messages in those specific channels in order to repost or log them inside the same server. Selene does not read DMs, and does not read channels it hasn't been given access to and configured for.
Commands used The content of slash commands run against the bot, so it can respond — for example a roster lookup or a top-list request.

Selene does not collect payment details, real names, addresses, or location data, and it does not build profiles of users across servers — each operator's instance only ever sees its own server.

04 What the developer receives

Automatically: nothing. A running copy of Selene sends no usage data, logs, or user information to the developer.

The developer only receives information in these situations, all of them initiated by you:

  • Buying Selene. Payment is handled by PayPal. The developer receives the payment confirmation PayPal provides — such as the buyer's name, email address, and the amount paid — so the purchase and download can be delivered. The developer never sees or stores card or bank details; PayPal's own privacy policy covers how PayPal handles them.
  • Asking for support. Messages you post or send in the Selene support Discord are visible to the developer and support helpers, like any Discord conversation. If you share screenshots or log files to help diagnose a problem, the developer will see whatever is in them.
  • Pilot and delivery messages. Contact details you use to receive your download (for example, the Discord account you open a ticket with) are used to deliver the software and the Buyer role.

Before sending logs or screenshots for support, check them for anything private. In particular, never share your selene.env file or your bot token — support will never ask for them.

05 Tokens & account credentials

Selene runs under the operator's own Discord bot account. The bot token is stored in the selene.env settings file on the operator's machine. It is never transmitted to the developer, and Selene does not send it anywhere other than to Discord itself, to sign the bot in.

Some faction features use a Minecraft alt account that the operator supplies. Any credentials for that account are likewise stored only in the operator's local configuration. Supplying an alt is the operator's choice and the operator's responsibility — including the risk that the game server may ban the alt ("your alt, your ban risk"). The developer never receives, stores, or uses operators' alt credentials.

06 How the data is used

Data processed by Selene is used only to provide the bot's features inside the operator's own server: rosters, leaderboards, faction boards, logs, mirrors, and command responses. It is not used for advertising, not used for marketing profiles, and not combined across different operators' servers.

Information the developer receives directly (payments and support messages) is used only to deliver Selene, provide support, and keep basic sales records.

07 Sharing & third parties

The developer does not sell, rent, or share personal data with third parties — and in practice holds almost none, for the reasons above.

Selene's operation does involve services run by others, each under its own privacy policy:

  • Discord — the bot connects to Discord using the operator's bot account. Everything the bot sends or receives passes through Discord, as with any bot.
  • PayPal — processes purchases of Selene.
  • The Minecraft servers the operator connects to — gameplay data the bot reads or displays originates there, under those servers' own rules.

Within a Discord server, what Selene posts (boards, logs, mirrors, leaderboards) is visible to whoever can see those channels. The operator chooses the channels and their permissions.

08 Retention & deletion

Because the data lives with the operator, so does its retention:

  • Data in an operator's instance stays in their selene.db until the operator removes it. Deleting that file deletes the bot's stored data for that server.
  • If you're a member of a server running Selene and want your linked IGN or stats removed, ask that server's operator — they are the one who can delete it. The developer cannot delete data from a machine they have no access to.
  • Uninstalling Selene and deleting its folder removes the software and its local data from the operator's machine entirely.
  • Payment records held by PayPal, and messages in the support Discord, are retained under those platforms' own policies and ordinary sales-record needs.

09 Security

Selene's design keeps data exposure small by keeping data local. Operators are responsible for securing their own machine and files — in particular, keeping their bot token private. Anyone holding a bot token controls that bot, so treat selene.env like a password: don't post it, don't send it to anyone (including anyone claiming to be support), and reset the token in the Discord Developer Portal if it's ever exposed.

No software can promise perfect security, and Selene is provided as-is; but no design decision in Selene sends operator or member data to the developer or any third party beyond the services listed in section 07.

10 Children

Selene is sold to operators who are old enough to enter a purchase agreement, and it runs inside Discord, which sets its own minimum age for accounts. Selene does not knowingly collect information from children beyond what Discord itself provides to any bot in a server. Server operators are responsible for using Selene in line with Discord's terms for their community.
